# Dual U Courtyard Estate — Buildable Project Brief

## Control note

This brief converts the uploaded concept board into a planning-ready project brief. The image is treated as a source concept board, not construction documentation. Items directly visible in the board are marked **Source image**. Items inferred from the project vision are marked **Client vision**. Items proposed for buildability are marked **Planning target**. Items that cannot be confirmed from the image are marked **TBD**.

This is not legal, zoning, civil engineering, architectural, accessibility-code, lending, or construction-pricing advice. Use it to brief licensed professionals and request site-specific written opinions.

## Executive project thesis

Build a luxury, ADA-forward, multi-generational family compound organized as a dual-U courtyard estate with three outdoor courts, 8+ bedrooms, 8.5+ baths, and four independent or semi-independent living zones. The design should preserve the emotional vision of a resort-grade family sanctuary while disciplining it into a parcel-filtering, schematic-design, pricing, financing, permitting, and operations plan.

The core real-estate question is not just “Can we design this?” It is “Can we find land where this massing, unit logic, driveway, utilities, septic or sewer, fire access, stormwater, and zoning position are all legal, financeable, insurable, and maintainable?”

- **Primary planning assumption:** 8+ bedrooms, 8.5+ baths, one main shared central house volume, two U-shaped residential wings, three courtyards, and four independent or semi-independent household zones.

- **Unit logic must be legally vetted early.** “Four independent living units” may be legal as one family compound, accessory suites, guest suites, caretaker quarters, two-family, multifamily, condo, or prohibited multifamily depending on parcel zoning.
- **Target dimensions below are planning targets, not extracted construction dimensions, unless marked source-visible.**

### Four-unit / duplex-triplex-quadplex configuration logic

| Configuration | Description | Pros | Risks / gating issue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single-family compound with four household zones | One legal dwelling with suites, lounges, laundries, and possibly limited wet bars | Simplest if zoning only allows single family | Full kitchens, separate meters, rental use, or separate entrances may trigger multifamily issues. |
| Main house + accessory suite(s) | Primary dwelling plus one or more accessory living areas | Supports elder/caregiver use | ADU rules are highly local and may limit size, occupancy, kitchens, parking, and rental. |
| Legal two-family / duplex | Left and right wings or central split configured as two legal units | Financing and resale clarity if permitted | May conflict with estate aesthetic and shared central core. |
| Triplex / quadplex | Four fully independent units around shared courtyard core | Best for multi-branch family governance | Most zoning-sensitive; may require multifamily zoning, fire separation, parking, sprinklers, separate utilities, and higher code burden. |
| Condo / family LLC / trust ownership | Legal/financial structure separates use rights without illegal units | Useful for estate planning | Requires attorney, lender, tax, insurance, and governance documents. |

## 3. Missing-information list

- Who are the primary residents now, and who is expected in 5, 10, and 20 years?

- Is the goal one family home, four legal units, rental capacity, caregiver quarters, or future resale flexibility?

## 4. Site due-diligence checklist

Use this as the land-filtering gate before falling in love with a parcel.

### Jurisdiction and zoning

- Confirm exact municipality and reviewing agencies: village, town, county, state, special district, utility district, fire district, and health department.
- Pull the current zoning map, zoning text, parcel card, survey, tax map, prior permits, certificates of occupancy, and open violations.
- Confirm principal use: single-family, two-family, multifamily, accessory dwelling, caretaker quarters, guest house, home occupation, greenhouse, workshop, and pool.
- Confirm dimensional controls: lot area, frontage, width, setbacks, height, stories, FAR, lot coverage, impervious coverage, accessory-structure placement, parking, lighting, fencing, and walls.
- Confirm whether the project is as-of-right, special permit, site plan, variance, subdivision, or rezoning.
- Confirm whether the desired unit logic would be treated as illegal apartments, legal accessory suites, or regulated multifamily.
- For the Farmingdale/Long Island example, start with the Incorporated Village of Farmingdale code/building department if the parcel is inside the Village, and the relevant town/county agencies if outside it. Current official Village resources point owners to local building and zoning regulations, and the Village zoning chapter is separately published online. Sources checked: Village Building Department and Chapter 600 Zoning pages.

### Septic, sewer, water, and health department

- Confirm sewer connection availability, connection fees, and capacity.
- If septic is required, complete soils, perc/deep-hole testing, bedroom-count capacity, reserve area, setbacks from wells/water bodies, and advanced-treatment requirements.
- Confirm potable water source, pressure, flow, meter size, irrigation allowance, and fire-suppression needs.
- Confirm backflow, water-softening, filtration, and greenhouse irrigation requirements.

### Driveway, access, and fire/life safety

- Confirm legal frontage and curb-cut approval.
- Model driveway width, turning, grade, bridge/culvert loading, gate setbacks, emergency vehicle access, and address visibility.
- Confirm fire-apparatus turnaround, hydrant distance, fire-flow, sprinkler triggers, fire lanes, and roof access.
- Separate family arrival, guest parking, accessible van parking, service deliveries, trash/recycling, and emergency access where possible.

### Stormwater, floodplain, and environmental

- Order boundary, topographic, tree, wetland, and utility surveys.
- Check FEMA floodplain, local flood maps, coastal constraints, wetlands, buffers, streams, ponds, drainage easements, and high groundwater.
- Prepare preliminary impervious-area budget for roofs, courtyards, driveway, terraces, pool deck, greenhouse, and outbuildings.
- Confirm stormwater detention, infiltration, drywells, rain gardens, overflow routing, snow storage, and maintenance responsibilities.

### Utilities and resilience

- Confirm electric service capacity for estate HVAC, EV charging, greenhouse, pool equipment, elevators, medical equipment, workshop, generator, and battery.
- Confirm natural gas or propane availability.
- Plan telecom redundancy, whole-house Wi-Fi, security cameras, access control, and emergency communications.
- Plan backup power hierarchy: life-safety, medical equipment, refrigeration, heating/cooling refuge, well/septic pumps, network, gates, and garage doors.

### HOA / ARC / private controls

- Review declarations, covenants, architectural review rules, private-road agreements, maintenance obligations, rental restrictions, exterior-material rules, lighting limits, fencing, gates, outbuildings, animals, and greenhouse restrictions.

## 5. ADA / universal design checklist for private residential use

### Important applicability note

The project should be **ADA-forward** and **universal-design-forward**, but a private single-family residence is not automatically governed the same way as a public accommodation, commercial facility, workplace, or multifamily property. If any part of the estate becomes public, commercial, short-term rental, event, workplace, multifamily, staff housing, or otherwise regulated, different ADA, Fair Housing, building-code, fire-code, and local accessibility obligations may apply. The design team must obtain jurisdiction-specific code advice.

| Domain | Checklist |

| Bedrooms | Bed transfer space, reachable controls, night lighting, emergency call options, acoustic privacy, caregiver chair/sleep option in ADA suites. |
| Bathrooms | Roll-in/curbless showers in ADA suites and guest suite; blocking for grab bars; fold-down seat; handheld shower; thermostatic valves; non-slip flooring; accessible storage; emergency call. |
| Toilets | Adequate side transfer clearance where required by user need, comfort-height options, blocking, bidet/washlet power, privacy plus caregiver access. |
| Vanities | Knee clearance where required, anti-scald protection, reachable storage, mirror visibility seated/standing. |
| Kitchen | Accessible island seating and knee space; reachable appliances; induction cooking; pull-out shelves; drawer storage; appliance garages; task lighting; seated prep area. |

## 6. Planning-level cost model

### Cost philosophy

This concept is an estate-scale custom project. The danger is fake precision. A responsible cost model should be built in layers, with explicit assumptions, allowances, alternates, contingencies, and escalation.

### Planning categories

| Cost category | Scope included | Primary cost drivers | Confidence before site selection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Land acquisition | Parcel purchase, due diligence, legal, title, closing | Location, acreage, zoning, privacy, sewer, existing structures | Low |
| Pre-development soft costs | Survey, geotech, zoning counsel, architect, civil, landscape, MEP, structural, accessibility consultant, estimator, owner’s rep | Jurisdiction complexity, variance path, revisions | Medium-low |
| Entitlements / approvals | Filing fees, hearings, environmental reviews, traffic/fire review, HOA/ARC | Unit count, setbacks, stormwater, neighbors, special permits | Low |
| Site work | Clearing, grading, driveway, utilities, septic/sewer, stormwater, retaining walls, gates, lighting | Soil, groundwater, topography, driveway length, stormwater | Low |
| Building shell | Foundation, structure, roof, exterior walls, windows/doors, insulation | U-shaped footprint, glass area, roof complexity, black cladding durability | Medium-low |
| Interiors | Framing, drywall, flooring, millwork, kitchens, baths, closets, hardware | 8.5+ baths, luxury finish level, custom cabinetry | Medium-low |
| Accessibility premium | Wider doors/halls, curbless showers, blocking, automated doors, accessible cabinetry, elevator-ready work | Number of fully accessible suites, automation level | Medium |
| MEP systems | HVAC, plumbing, electrical, lighting, low voltage, smart home, backup power | Estate size, glass, greenhouse, EV, generator, batteries, medical loads | Medium-low |
| Garage/workshop/storage | Garage, van bay, EV charging, workshop, storage, lifts if any | Bay count, conditioning, tools, outbuilding rules | Low |
| Landscape/courtyards | Hardscape, planting, irrigation, walls, water/fire features, exterior lighting | Three courtyards, mature planting, drainage, masonry | Low |
| Greenhouse/garden | Greenhouse, raised beds, utilities, controls, paths | Year-round conditioning, glass, automation, heating | Low |
| Pool/water/fire features | Pool, spa, ponds, streams, fire pits, equipment | Safety, structure, waterproofing, energy, maintenance | Low |
| Furniture, fixtures, equipment | Furniture, window treatments, appliances, AV, medical/adaptive equipment | Luxury level, custom pieces, smart systems | Low |
| Insurance, taxes, carrying costs | Construction insurance, builder’s risk, taxes, interest, utilities | Schedule length, loan structure, jurisdiction | Low |
| Contingency | Owner contingency, design contingency, construction contingency | Unknowns, market volatility, design maturity | Must be explicit |
| Escalation | Inflation between concept, bid, and build | Schedule and market | Must be explicit |

### Early budget discipline

- Establish **three scopes** before design: minimum viable compound, target estate, and dream estate.
- Carry **site-work contingency separately** from house contingency.
- Carry **allowances** for kitchens, baths, windows, cladding, landscape, greenhouse, smart home, generator/battery, and accessibility equipment.
- Price alternates for pool, water feature, greenhouse conditioning, elevator installation vs elevator-ready shaft, solar/battery, and fourth legal unit buildout.
- Do not accept a single lump-sum “estate cost” without inclusions, exclusions, assumptions, unit count treatment, and escalation date.

## 8. Builder / architect RFP draft

### RFP title

Request for Qualifications and Proposal: Dual U Courtyard Estate — ADA-Forward Multi-Generational Family Compound

### Project summary

The owner seeks an architect-led design team and/or luxury custom builder/design-build team for a dual-U courtyard estate on an estate-scale parcel. The concept includes 8+ bedrooms, 8.5+ baths, three courtyards, ADA-forward universal design, multi-generational living, greenhouse/garden integration, garage/storage/workshop, accessible van parking, EV charging, and possible legal configuration as one compound or multiple independent living zones depending on zoning.

### Required respondent qualifications

- Luxury custom residential estate experience.
- Demonstrated universal design / aging-in-place / accessible residential design experience.
- Experience with complex site planning, long driveways, stormwater, septic or sewer coordination, and high-end landscape integration.
- Experience with multi-generational family compounds, guest suites, ADUs, two-family, or multifamily approvals where relevant.
- Ability to coordinate architect, civil engineer, landscape architect, structural engineer, MEP engineer, accessibility consultant, estimator, zoning counsel, and interior designer.
- Transparent estimating and change-order process.
- References from projects of comparable scale and complexity.

### Scope requested

Respondents should propose services for:

1. Concept review and feasibility.
2. Site selection support or selected-site due diligence.
3. Zoning and code coordination.
4. Schematic design, including measured floor plans, site plan, massing, and accessibility matrix.
5. Planning estimate with alternates.
6. Design development and construction documents.
7. Permitting support.
8. Builder procurement or construction delivery.
9. Construction administration / owner representation.
10. Commissioning, punch list, and operations handoff.

### Required proposal contents

- Team organization chart and responsible lead for each discipline.
- Relevant project examples with budget range, size, delivery method, and references.
- Accessibility/universal design approach.
- Zoning and entitlement approach for independent-living-unit logic.
- Site/civil risk-management approach.
- Preliminary schedule by phase.
- Fee structure and reimbursables.
- Estimating approach and expected estimate milestones.
- Construction delivery options: competitive bid, negotiated GMP, design-build, construction manager, or hybrid.
- Assumptions, exclusions, and owner decisions needed.
- Insurance coverage and licensing.

### RFP questions to force clarity

- How would you prevent this from becoming an illegal multifamily use if zoning does not allow four units?
- What information do you need before giving a credible cost range?
- How do you detail zero-step courtyard thresholds in freeze/thaw climates?
- How do you coordinate 42-inch doors, 48-inch halls, and 60-inch turning radii without inflating the plan inefficiently?
- How do you manage acoustic comfort in a two-story great room connected to bedroom wings?
- What is your change-order prevention process?
- What allowances are most likely to blow up on this project?
- How would you phase or value-engineer the dream without destroying the core concept?
